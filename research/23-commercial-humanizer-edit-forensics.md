# Commercial Humanizer Edit Forensics: What the Tools Actually Change, and Which Changes Are Noise

*Research report, 2026-09-07. Companion to reports 03 (who the tools are), 12 (that they leave artifacts) and 13 (GPTZero deltas). This report diffs published input → output pairs edit by edit, measures each tool's edit mix, and asks whether the tools that pass GPTZero pass because of noise. Web-search budget ran out mid-task, so several sources (Reddit, Medium, Twixify, undetectable.ai's own site) were reached only through mirrors, earlier reports, or not at all; those gaps are marked.*

---

## 0. Findings up front

1. **Noise is not what makes the passing tools pass GPTZero.** Across every independent 2025–2026 test collected here, the tool with the *highest* injected-error rate (StealthWriter, 4.2 grammar issues per 1,000 words, deliberate misspellings of proper nouns) is flagged **100% AI by GPTZero** in four separate tests, while the tool with the *lowest* error rate among the evaders (Undetectable AI, 1.8 per 1,000) has the highest GPTZero pass rates (Karr 2025: >96% false-negative rate; IntellectualLead Feb 2026: 98% bypass; ProofreaderPro Aug 2026: 94%). Fluent paraphrasers that change *nothing distributional* (QuillBot, Grammarly, GPTinf, HumanizeAI.pro, Surfer, Netus) fail at 82–100% AI. Pass rate tracks **distributional distance from instruct-model prose**, not error count.

2. **Noise does buy passes against perplexity detectors, and that is where the folklore comes from.** Three natural experiments show it cleanly. Gold Penguin's Undetectable output with "1.54°C" corrupted to "1 page" and "hottest" deleted twice scored **0% AI on Sapling**. Essaydone fixed Undetectable's "35 missing commas" with Grammarly and ZeroGPT jumped **7.3% → 44% AI** while GPTZero was unreported. Essaydone repaired Ryne's output and ZeroGPT went **58% → 100% AI while GPTZero stayed at 100% both times**. Errors move ZeroGPT, Sapling and Copyleaks; they do not move GPTZero. StealthWriter's own UI admits the dose–response: "Lower levels have better readability and grammar, but can sometimes get detected by advanced detectors."

3. **What the GPTZero-passing tools actually do is three things, and two are incompatible with academic prose.** (a) *Lexical de-academization*: Karr's paired measurement of Undetectable AI v11 on 642 published abstracts (2,568 pairs) shows long-token ratio 0.184 → 0.138 (−25%, decreased in 88% of pairs), Academic Word List ratio 0.157 → 0.121 (−23%), type-token ratio 0.700 → 0.605 (decreased in 93% of pairs). (b) *Expansion, not fragmentation*: words per sentence **23.6 → 28.4 (+20%)**, word count **180 → 229 (+27%)**, short-sentence share barely moved (0.063 → 0.072). Karr's extreme case went from 129 to 311 words and 25.8 to 62.2 words per sentence while both detectors fell from 1.000 to 0.000. Karr's own gloss: "evasion suppresses detector-salient surface cues rather than uniformly fragmenting syntax." The tools do not shatter sentences; they unpack noun phrases into clauses and chain them with *which / while / because / yet*. (c) *Register and voice injection*: "the findings indicate" → "we can see that" (ProofreaderPro), "the imagination" → "our imagination", "The folks who opposed the measure" (StealthGPT on a news passage), added "dare we say", "Simply put", rhetorical questions, contractions. Only (b)'s syntactic re-expression, done without the length inflation, is compatible with our quality target.

4. **The edit mix is tool-specific and stable enough to be a fingerprint, which is exactly why Pangram, GPTZero and Turnitin now catch it.** Undetectable AI's 2024 model stripped possessive apostrophes ("Apollos", "humanitys", "todays"), deleted list and introductory commas, and inserted a comma before a preposition roughly every 25 words ("talents, in mythology", "excitement, about"). Its 2025–26 model dropped those tics and instead flattens every subordinate clause into coordinated declaratives with *yet / but / because*. Humbot injects keyboard typos ("previojsly", "eforts", "Thoughf"), ALL-CAPS opening sentences, profanity and hallucinated proper nouns ("Michelle Obama"). HIX Bypass emits non-words ("maholics", "chanceless", "spackling"). StealthGPT adds opinion markers and fabricated specifics (Marc Andreessen, trucking jobs, "The Council of Mayo Clinic in 2017", a "Whitney Mangus"). Pangram's anonymous Humanizer_1 turned 164 hyphens in 194 texts into zero. DAMAGE's independent per-tool audit (Table 9 of arXiv 2501.03437) matches our diffs tool for tool: Ghost AI "splits every sentence into single-clause statements"; BypassGPT "leans heavily on dictionary lookup paraphrasing… no typos"; StealthWriter "grammar, punctuation, capitalization issues, generally one per paragraph"; Undetectable AI "elementary school level. Introduces typos"; HumanizeAI.io "fictional citations, series of question marks"; StealthGPT "output closely matches style of original text."

5. **Synonym substitution is the dominant edit in every tool (4–23 per 100 words) and it is almost never the *rarer* word.** Across 25 diffed pairs the substitutions run 80–90% toward the *plainer, flatter* word (embodies → represents, sweltered → endured, insidious → devious, alarming evidence of accelerating global warming → concerning signs of climate change). Tortured rare synonyms ("gastronomical misbeliefs", "counterfeit consciousness") are a minority signature of a few tools and of aggressive settings. The damage from synonymy is register collapse and metaphor flattening, not obscurity.

6. **Meaning change is frequent and is the real quality cost.** Roughly 1–2 meaning-altering edits per 100 words in the L3 tools: "no significant correlation" inverted, "No adapter" → "None of the adapters", "same-day" → "one-day", "the market grows to $95.93B" → "a $95.93 billion expansion", "On the other hand the less fortunate" → "On the hand the fortunate", "Another danger" → "The main threat", "2023" → "this year". Hallucinated content (a two-time world champion, a recent biography, "Abortions are for them something…") appears at 0.7–1.8 per 100 words in Humbot, HIX and StealthGPT.

7. **Detectors have now trained on precisely these tics.** GPTZero's paraphrase class is *conditional* (AI first, then paraphrased) and its published example shows comma splices, a missing space after a period, a dropped apostrophe and a fragment: the 2024 Undetectable signature. Pangram's artifact list is hyphen→comma, thin spaces, stray capitals, tense mixing, altered quotations, hallucinations. Turnitin's 2026 model (per a vendor summary) targets "sentence lengths that oscillate too dramatically, vocabulary that shifts register too frequently" and "transitions… syntactically correct but semantically unnatural". Russell et al.'s experts caught o1-Pro-humanized text through *surviving* AI vocabulary and an over-use of titles (Dr., Prof.) before names. The noise playbook is not merely useless against GPTZero; it is now a positive feature.

8. **The no-noise path exists and is the one GPTZero is weakest against.** Russell et al.'s guidebook-prompted o1-Pro (no injected errors, only style instructions) cut GPTZero to **46.7% TPR** while Pangram's humanizer model held **96.7%**; Jabarian & Imas's StealthGPT run (DAMAGE L1, fluent) put GPTZero's false-negative rate at 25–77%; Van Vlasselaer et al. humanised the AI sections of 4,000–7,000-word theses with a plain GPT-4o prompt asking for "modest hedging or reflection (e.g., 'This suggests that…', 'One might argue…')" while "preserving all references and technical terminology", and GPTZero was correct on **1 of 40** (Pangram 37 of 40); a prompt-evolution loop with no noise cut Pangram itself from 94.7% to **24.1%** recall (arXiv 2606.25152); base-model iterative paraphrase (report 13) reaches ~100% human. GPTZero's own April 2026 advice for lowering a score, varied structure, concrete anecdotes, personal perspective, contractions, is a register recipe, not a noise recipe. Our constraint is to take the register-neutral half of it.

9. **Perplexity has to land *in* the human band, and the two failure modes are symmetric.** Fluent paraphrasers fail because they push perplexity *below* human: in the Adversarial Paraphrasing study a simple Llama-3 paraphrase scored PPL 9.28 against human 15.02 and original AI 14.94 ("artificially low"), while the detector-guided version that evaded eight detectors landed at 14.3–17.1, i.e., *at* human. Noise tools fail the other way: HMGC-style word substitution adds +45 to +74 PPL (TH-Bench), and the RL-trained AuthorMist over-shoots to PPL 300–400 against a human 50–75. Prompt-only LLM rewriting sits in a third bad place: PPL unchanged, Flesch *down* 13–14 points (more formal), and evasion nil (AUC rises to 0.939). Report 12's perplexity-band gate is therefore two-sided, and the target is the human distribution, not "higher".

## 1. Method and evidence base

Thirty-four input → output pairs (or output-only excerpts with the input paraphrased by the reviewer) were collected from: Gold Penguin's Undetectable, Humbot and HIX Bypass reviews (2024 tests, pages updated June 2026; Sapling and GPTZero); IntellectualLead's five-tool head-to-head on one robotics paragraph (Feb 2026; Originality and GPTZero); Jabarian & Imas's Appendix F (StealthGPT on news passages); GPTZero's own StealthWriter, Undetectable and paraphrase-detection posts; Pangram's "(Slop)² Problem" post (five anonymized humanizers); DAMAGE's quoted L3 excerpts; RAID's Table 18 attack examples; Soto et al. and Beemo's paired examples of academic attacks and expert edits; vendor landing-page demos (WriteHuman, JustDone, HumanizeAI.io, Undetectable); and Trustpilot/review quotations. Each pair was diffed by hand and every edit assigned to one of 19 categories (§2). Counts are normalized per 100 *input* words (per 100 output words where the input was truncated by the source). Sample sizes are small (19–350 words per pair, 100–900 words per tool), so treat rates as order-of-magnitude, not estimates.

Quantitative quality data come from Karr (arXiv 2608.11256, Table 22), TH-Bench (arXiv 2503.08708), DAMAGE (arXiv 2501.03437), Beemo (arXiv 2411.04032), Adversarial Paraphrasing (arXiv 2506.07001), fast.io's 2026 reviews, ProofreaderPro's LanguageTool-scored academic benchmark, DetectionDrama's Rephrasy test and Essaydone's Grammarly-scored tests. GPTZero pass/fail data come from every dated 2025–2026 test found (§4, Table 3).

Three caveats carry over from report 03. Most reviews are competitor-authored. Most run one passage. GPTZero shipped at least five models across the window (3.7b Aug 2025, 3.15b Dec 2025, "2025-12-18-base", 4.3b Feb 2026, 4.4b mid-2026), so a January 2025 pass says little about September 2026.

Reading the table column-wise:

- **SYN is universal and mostly downward.** Only Humbot, HIX and Pangram's Humanizer_1 produce the tortured-rare substitution at any rate (1–2 per 100). The bulk of substitution in every tool is toward the more frequent word.
- **PUN and ERR-S separate the tool families cleanly.** Undetectable 2024 (punctuation corruption, no spelling errors), Humbot (keyboard typos, no comma games), HIX (non-words), StealthWriter (proper-noun misspellings plus apostrophe deletion). Each is a distinct, deterministic post-processing layer and is therefore trivially learnable.
- **FUN and UNP are the signature of the 2025–26 generation.** Undetectable's newer model and StealthWriter both convert punctuation-carried structure (dashes, semicolons, participial clauses) into explicit *which / while / because / yet* chains, and both nominalize or de-nominalize aggressively. This is what produces Karr's +20% words per sentence with no increase in short sentences.
- **SPL is rare.** Sentence-count preservation is the norm in every sentence-level tool (Undetectable 2024, StealthWriter, Humbot, QuillBot). The "shatter into short sentences" pattern appears only in vendor demos (WriteHuman, JustDone). The folklore that humanizers work by burstiness is not borne out by their actual output; they work by lexis and clause structure.
- **1P/2P, RQ, HDG cluster in StealthGPT, Humbot and the demos**, i.e., the tools that behave like a prompted generative LLM rather than a paraphraser.
- **MCH is 1–2 per 100 words in every L3 tool and in StealthGPT.** For a 2,000-word paper that is 20–40 factual or logical corruptions.

Three things in this table matter for us. First, the grade-level collapse is named explicitly for five tools (Undetectable, WriteHuman, Surfer, AIHumanizer.com, and by implication Phrasly), and it is the tools with that note that show the highest independent GPTZero pass rates. Second, DAMAGE's authors distinguish two architectures by their grounding: "humanizers built on LLMs tend to be more weakly grounded in the original text, while rules-based humanizers that do synonym replacement tend to be more strongly grounded in the original text." The weakly grounded LLM tools are the ones that pass; the strongly grounded dictionary tools (BypassGPT, Twixify, GPTinf) are the ones that fail. Third, only one tool is credited with sentence shattering (Ghost AI), and Pangram catches Ghost AI 100% of the time. Burstiness-by-fragmentation is both rare among the tools and useless against the detectors that matter.

### 3.2 What the numbers say

**Undetectable AI's damage is register, not grammar.** Its grammar-issue rate (1.8/1,000) is *below* the human student baseline of 2.45 per 100 words (report 09). What Karr measures is a wholesale shift of the vocabulary distribution away from academic English: a quarter of the long words gone, a quarter of the AWL words gone, lexical diversity down 13%, and the text 27% longer. That is a Doctorate-mode setting on published abstracts. ProofreaderPro's editors score the same effect as academic tone 3/5 and 5.4/10. This is the single most important measurement in this report: the best-evading mainstream tool passes by writing *less like a scholar*, and it inflates length to do so.

**StealthWriter's damage is grammar, and it does not pay.** 4.2 issues per 1,000, meaning preservation 6.8, ProofreaderPro 8.5/25, "Register degraded by inserted errors", and GPTZero 100% AI in fast.io, GPTZero's own review (Feb 2026, Ghost 4.6 Mini), and three of four IntellectualLead samples. Its higher aggressiveness levels do clear ZeroGPT and Turnitin (DetectionDrama: level 5 → ZeroGPT 100%, level 10 → 0%; "10 wrecks readability").

**Readability drops in most tools, by different mechanisms.** Rephrasy's Flesch fell 3.6 points because sentences lengthened. Undetectable's Doctorate mode lengthened sentences by 20%. Humbot and HIX inflate length 30–40% with filler. WriteHuman's demo shortens and simplifies (Flesch up). There is no consistent "dumbing down" in the Flesch sense; there is a consistent *lexical* dumbing down (AWL and long-token loss) alongside *syntactic* bloat. For a grader that is the worst combination: the vocabulary of a weaker student in sentences that are harder to read.

**Zero grammar errors is what the fluent failures have in common.** QuillBot, Grammarly, GPTinf and HumanizeAI.pro produce clean output and fail. But StealthGPT (DAMAGE L1, no injected errors) passes GPTZero about half the time, and Russell et al.'s guidebook o1-Pro (no errors) passes 53%. Cleanliness is not the problem; sameness is. The Adversarial Paraphrasing numbers say where the sameness lives: a plain Llama-3 paraphrase drove perplexity from 14.9 to 9.3, *below* human, and evaded nothing; the detector-guided version that evaded eight detectors sat at 14.3–17.1, on top of the human 15.0. Fluent paraphrasers fail because they make text more predictable than a person would, not because they are clean.

**Human editors compress; humanizers expand.** Beemo's experts, told to edit 20–40% of the words, cut machine text by 15% and dropped detector AUROC by up to 22 points. Every commercial tool measured here inflates: Karr +27%, Humbot +30–40%, StealthGPT +30–60% on the Jabarian & Imas passages, HumanizeAI.io +22%. The direction of the length change is itself a humanizer signature and the opposite of what a real reviser does.

### 4.1 The correlation

Plot damage against pass rate and the sign is wrong for the noise hypothesis:

- **Highest error injection → lowest GPTZero pass.** StealthWriter (4.2/1,000, deliberate typos) 100% AI in four tests. Humbot (typos, caps, profanity) 98% AI on Originality's run, 0% pass in two of Gold Penguin's three. HIX (non-words) 100% AI on Originality's run. BypassGPT (word salad) 89% AI.
- **Lowest error rate among evaders → highest GPTZero pass.** Undetectable AI, 1.8/1,000, is the best-evidenced GPTZero evader (Karr, ProofreaderPro, IntellectualLead), *and* the hardest tool for Pangram (90.3% caught, lowest of 20), *and* DAMAGE's lowest quality tier. Its damage is not noise; it is register and vocabulary.
- **Zero damage with zero distributional change → zero pass.** QuillBot, Grammarly, GPTinf, HumanizeAI.pro, Surfer, Smodin, Netus: fluent synonym-and-reorder paraphrase, 82–100% AI on GPTZero, 99–100% caught by Pangram.
- **Zero noise with real distributional change → the best pass rates.** StealthGPT (L1) ~50% on the largest independent sample; guidebook-prompted o1-Pro 53%; detector-trained SuperHumanizer 58–85%; base-model paraphrase ~100% (report 13).

The one apparent counter-example is instructive. Undetectable's 2024 model *did* rely on punctuation corruption, and it *did* pass GPTZero in Gold Penguin's 2024 runs (1–19% AI). GPTZero's November 2024 paraphrase-detection post then published, as its canonical example of a bypasser, a passage carrying exactly that fingerprint (comma before preposition, missing space after period, dropped apostrophe, fragment). By 2025 Gold Penguin reports Undetectable's outputs are "cleaner… I wouldn't have spotted anything wrong", and Karr's v11 measurement shows a purely distributional strategy. The market leader appears to have learned in production that punctuation noise had become a liability against GPTZero and moved to lexical and syntactic distance. That is the trajectory we should expect every surviving tool to follow, and it is the trajectory GPTZero's "AI Paraphrased" head and Pangram's humanizer head were built to follow it into.

## 5. What GPTZero, Pangram and Turnitin have learned to catch

**GPTZero.** The paraphrase judgement is two-step and conditional: "first: detecting if the modified text is originally AI-written, and only then analyzing if the content has been altered using other writing tools to appear human" (Nov 2024). Its published bypasser example is the artifact list in miniature: "Income inequality remains a issue marked by variations, in how wealth is spread across various societal groups.This economic and social problem impacts individuals well being. Also hinders both progress and community unity." GPTZero's gloss: paraphrasers "heavily alter both syntax and vocabulary with permissible grammatical inaccuracies", producing "comma splices… punctuation formatting errors and sentence fragments". Model 3.15b (Dec 2025) raised sensitivity enough that Verva reports 2013 human prose relabelled "100% AI… paraphrased AI", a false-positive risk we can exploit only by not looking paraphrased at all. Model 4.3b (Feb 2026) reports 91.8% recall on 1,000 texts through "12+ dedicated bypasser services" at 0.21% FPR (vendor-run). AI Patterns (Aug 2026) enumerates ten rhetorical habits that lexical rewriting leaves intact; GPTZero says they are interpretability, not score, but JustDone's demo output containing "not a stylistic choice. It is the standard." shows humanizers *adding* them. GPTZero's own "how to lower your AI score" (Apr 2026) lists what it rewards: varied structure, specific anecdotes, contractions, personal opinion, concrete detail. Two of those five are register moves we cannot make in academic prose.

**Pangram.** The most concrete published artifact list, now confirmed with per-humanizer attributions: hyphen → comma find-and-replace (Humanizer_1: 164 hyphens → 0), stray capitals ("Government's", "White Paper", "Prison Reform", "Senior E.U. Official"; Humanizer_2), missing spaces after terminal punctuation ("question!Yeah"; Humanizer_5), U+2009 thin spaces, tortured phrases ("gastronomical misbeliefs", "counterfeit consciousness", "snipping edges"), tone-deaf synonyms ("youngster"), inverted register ("Dear Team" → "To Whom It May Concern"), altered quotations, tense mixing, hallucinated facts and even promotional insertions. Pangram's summary rule, "the more readable/fluent the humanizer text is, the more likely it is to be detected by Pangram", is true *of Pangram*, whose humanizer head was trained with humanization as an invariance (report 12 §3.2). It is the reverse of GPTZero's weakness, which is fluent register-preserving rewrites. A pipeline that is tuned on GPTZero alone will drift toward exactly what Pangram catches.

**Turnitin.** Aug 2025 bypasser detection "trained on the signals and patterns of leading humanizers"; Feb 2026 recall update. The only description of the signals is second-hand (SupWriter, Apr 2026): "over-correction patterns: sentence lengths that oscillate too dramatically, vocabulary that shifts register too frequently"; "semantic drift markers"; "transitions… syntactically correct but semantically unnatural". Treat as plausible but unverified. It matches report 10's finding that human sentence-length variation is a band (CV 0.42–0.60) and that alternating long/short is anomalous.

**Human experts (Russell et al.).** After o1-Pro humanization, expert annotators still reached 100% majority-vote accuracy, using surviving AI vocabulary (42% of correct explanations), and a new tell: humanized articles "use titles for these people (e.g., Dr., Prof.) much more frequently than in human-written articles". Humanization moved the tells; it did not remove them.

**Originality.ai.** Per-tool accuracy 80–97% (Sept 2025 models) with no artifact taxonomy published. Its Humbot verdict, "runs every third or fourth word through a thesaurus, leaving users with long-winded, wordy, and confusing sentence structures", is the best one-line description of the L2/L3 paraphrasers.

**What survives every edit type (the academic detectors).** Two results bound what any edit mix can achieve. Soto et al.: under every attack tested (detector-DPO, DIPPER, TinyStyler, GPT-4o-mini paraphrase and style-aware paraphrase) a style-embedding detector held ~95 AUROC while perplexity detectors fell to 35–65, because "generic optimization fails to converge to a valid human stylistic fingerprint"; the one thing that broke it was paraphrasing toward a *specific human author's* style. Triospect: across the Humanize-16K attacks including the three commercial tools, semantic-content embeddings varied by only 6.1% while POS structure varied 23.1%, so a content-origin detector recovers most of the loss. The edit taxonomy in §2 is entirely expression-level; none of it touches these two signals, which is why report 12's conclusion (change what is said, and carry a style term in the reward) stands.

## 6. Conclusion for us

**Is noise what makes the passing tools pass?** No. Noise (misspellings, dropped apostrophes, comma games, missing spaces, ALL-CAPS, non-words) is what made tools pass *perplexity* detectors in 2023–24 and still clears ZeroGPT and Sapling today; it has zero or negative effect on GPTZero 3.15b+ and is a named positive feature for Pangram and GPTZero's paraphrase head. The tool with the most noise (StealthWriter) is the most reliably caught by GPTZero.

**What is?** Three things, in descending order of evidence: (1) lexical distribution shift away from instruct-model academic English (Karr: −25% long tokens, −23% AWL), (2) syntactic re-expression that converts punctuation-carried structure into explicit clause chains and changes nominal density, and (3) added voice, stance and content (first person, opinion markers, specifics, some fabricated). The passing tools buy (1) and (3) at a register cost that makes their output read as a weaker student, and buy (2) at a +20–27% length cost. Detector-in-the-loop selection (SuperHumanizer, WriteHuman Enhanced, StealthGPT's toggle) multiplies whatever the base transform does.

**Dose and quality cost, from the passing tools' own numbers.** Undetectable v11 spends about 25% of its academic vocabulary, 13% of its lexical diversity and a 27% length inflation to buy a >96% GPTZero pass on abstracts, and is scored 3/5 on academic tone and 2.67% coherence win-rate (DAMAGE L3) for it. StealthGPT spends about 1.7 meaning changes or fabrications per 100 words for a ~50% pass. Neither trade is available to us.

**Three design consequences** before the edit-by-edit mapping in §7. First, evaluate against Pangram and a perplexity detector as well as GPTZero, and require the pass to come from the same output: the tools' history shows that optimizing against one detector selects exactly the artifact another treats as a confession (noise passes ZeroGPT and fails GPTZero; fluency passes GPTZero and fails Pangram). Second, measure the Karr five on every output (long-token ratio, AWL ratio, type-token ratio, words per sentence, length ratio) and hold all five inside the input's band; that one gate would have rejected every Undetectable, Humbot and HIX output in this report. Third, budget the safe edits rather than maximize them: what the passing tools do, they do too much of, and the target is the human band on each feature, not the tool's rate.

### 7.2 Why the top tools pass: noise, rewriter, or iteration against a detector

The evidence supports one ordering and it decides the architecture.

**Noise: no, and declining.** Every noise-heavy tool is flagged by GPTZero in 2025–26 (StealthWriter 100% in four tests; Humbot 98% AI on Originality's run; HIX 100%; BypassGPT 89%), the only noise-heavy passes are against perplexity detectors, and the market leader visibly removed its punctuation-noise layer between its 2024 and 2025 models. Noise is a legacy layer aimed at the 2023 detector generation.

**A generative rewriter that moves the lexical and syntactic distribution: yes, this is the engine.** Undetectable AI's job posting ("fine-tune pre-trained LLMs… Hugging Face"), its versioned engine, its CEO's "fine-tuned on specific human writers", BypassGPT's "trained on over 200 million AI-generated texts… and human content", Phrasly's "trained on 500k+ real human articles", and DAMAGE's jailbreaks ("an LLM instructed to 'write at a college level'… 'rephrase the following text'") all say fine-tuned or prompted LLM. The measured output of the best passer is a distribution shift (Karr's −25% long tokens, −23% AWL, −13% TTR, +27% length), and DAMAGE's grounding observation (LLM tools "weakly grounded", dictionary tools "strongly grounded") maps exactly onto pass versus fail. The academic controls agree: a Llama-3 paraphrase landing at human perplexity evaded eight detectors; a base-model paraphraser reached 100% human; a GPT-4o prompt with hedges beat GPTZero on 39 of 40 theses. What the commercial rewriters lack is a fidelity and register constraint, which is why they pay with grade level.

**Iteration against a detector: yes, as the multiplier, not the engine.** SuperHumanizer "built… based on our own AI detector, as well as ZeroGPT and GPTZero"; WriteHuman's Enhanced model "updated September 3 for GPTZero and Pangram detection bypass rates"; StealthGPT's Turnitin-or-GPTZero toggle; Undetectable's eight-detector consensus and five rewrites per run (Phrasly); DAMAGE's own adversary was a fine-tuned GPT-4o optimised against a detector; GPTZero itself says it uses RL to find the prompts that fool it. Selection against a detector is how a mediocre transform becomes a 50–95% pass rate, and it is also how a tool inherits a detector-specific artifact (noise for ZeroGPT, fluency for GPTZero) that the *other* detector punishes.

**For us.** This is the architecture report 00 §6 already specifies and that `pipeline.py` already has the skeleton of: a rewriter (Stage 1, base or lightly fine-tuned checkpoint, not few-shot prompting of a raw base model, since `freeform` currently buys its low scores with content-overlap 0.01–0.15 and HIP's result says to fine-tune the base into a paraphraser instead), with the deterministic `transforms` as a post-generation scrub, best-of-N with engineered diversity, and detector-in-the-loop selection against an *ensemble* that includes Pangram-class and perplexity detectors. Three changes follow directly from this report: re-tighten `GATE_PRESETS[evade]` (its 0.30 overlap and ±40% length are the L3 tools' operating region); add the Karr-five register gate, the punctuation-class diff, the new-entity check, the error-density band and the two-sided perplexity band to `pipeline`; and add register-preservation, no-contraction and no-reader-address rules to `STYLE_CONTRACT`. None of the passing tools' *noise* belongs in the product. Their *rewriter* does, with the constraints they refuse to carry.
