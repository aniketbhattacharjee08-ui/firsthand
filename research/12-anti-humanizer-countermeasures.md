# Anti-Humanizer Countermeasures: What Betrays Humanized Text

*Research report, September 2026. Companion to reports 01–06; this one inverts the question. Reports 01–06 ask "what makes text look human." This report asks "what makes text look **humanized**," because humanized text is a separate, detectable third class and our product must not produce it.*

---

## 0. The central finding

Every major detector vendor now treats "AI text that has been run through a bypass tool" as its own class, not as a harder instance of "AI." GPTZero's internal taxonomy has five classes — Human, Mixed (Concatenated), Mixed (Polished), AI (Pure AI), **AI (Paraphrased)** — binarised only for benchmark reporting [1]. Pangram 4 ships an auxiliary humanizer head that distinguishes humanized AI from plain AI at 96.82% accuracy / 95.57% recall [2]. Turnitin's Chief Product Officer says the quiet part out loud: humanizers "leave a statistical trace that can be learned and detected" [3].

The mechanism is simple and should be assumed to be permanent. A humanizer is a *fixed transform*. Run it on 10,000 documents and it commits the same tics 10,000 times: the same synonym substitutions, the same punctuation find-and-replace, the same tokenizer tricks, the same register collapse. That regularity is far easier to learn than the diffuse fingerprint of an LLM. Pangram's data-centric fix — treat humanization as an *invariance* to learn rather than a domain to memorise, and label human documents that have been run through a humanizer as **human** — recovers 98.26% TPR at 5% FPR and generalises to humanizers never seen in training [4].

The strategic consequence for us: **the goal is not "beat the AI classifier." The goal is to produce text that carries no evidence of having been transformed at all.** A pipeline that lowers an AI score while leaving transform residue has moved the document from one flagged class to another flagged class.

## 1. GPTZero's Paraphraser Shield and the "AI (Paraphrased)" class

### 1.1 What is publicly documented

GPTZero's technology page describes a five-stage pipeline: input processing → end-to-end deep learning → sentence-by-sentence classifier → **Paraphraser Shield** → output. The Shield is described as defending "against tools looking to exploit AI detectors" and shielding "against common methods to bypass AI detection, such as paraphrasing and homoglyph attacks," concluding that even when "AI content has been altered to look more human-like, GPTZero can detect it" [5].

It is best understood as a **stage in the same model's pipeline plus a class head**, not a standalone product. Evidence: (a) the taxonomy places "AI (Paraphrased)" alongside "AI (Pure AI)" as sibling classes of one classifier [1]; (b) the shield sits *after* the sentence classifier in the documented pipeline [5]; (c) GPTZero describes the logic as two-step — "first: detecting if the modified text is originally AI-written, and only then analyzing if the content has been altered using other writing tools to appear human" [6]. So the paraphrase judgement is **conditional on an AI judgement**. Human text run through QuillBot should not become "AI (Paraphrased)"; AI text run through QuillBot should.

In the product UI, the label surfaces as "possible AI paraphrasing detected" at medium-or-high confidence in the Quick Scan result [7]. For our purposes the important consequence is that a document has three ways to fail, not one: flagged AI, flagged Mixed (Polished), or flagged AI (Paraphrased).

### 1.2 Training data and the provenance of "93.5%"

Two GPTZero posts carry the numbers, and they are different runs of the same benchmark family.

- **"Detecting AI-Humanized Text: How GPTZero Stays Ahead."** Dataset: 1,000 paraphrased examples, "equally distributed across paraphrasers," drawn from source documents generated by GPT-5, GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, Claude and Gemini across several domains. Trained/evaluated against **"12+ open-source and closed-source paraphrasing tools"**; the only tool named is the open-source "Temp paraphraser" (EMNLP 2025). Result: **GPTZero 93.5% AI recall**, Pangram v3 50.2%, Originality lite-102 57.3%, with 98%+ accuracy on the un-humanized source documents. The method is described only as looking for "deeper semantic and structural signals beyond surface form"; no architecture, sample counts or artifact list is disclosed [8].
- **The 2026 benchmarking post.** Same benchmark design (1,000 AI texts modified using "paraphrasing techniques and more than 12 dedicated bypasser services," spanning academic, scientific, essay, social and creative writing; sources GPT-5 / GPT-4o / GPT-4.1). Table 7: **GPTZero 4.3b — FPR 0.21%, recall 91.80%**; Pangram 3.2 — FPR 0.11%, recall 68.11%; Originality lite-102 — FPR 0.00%, recall 57.30% [1].

So 93.5% and 91.8% are the same claim at two model versions. **Both are vendor-run, on a vendor-chosen humanizer set, with the tool list undisclosed.** Provenance of the earlier "~95% with <0.1% false positive" beta figure: the 2024 paraphrase-detection announcement, which named Phrasly, Undetectable and QuillBot as tools it detects, without saying they were training data [6].

### 1.3 Independent numbers say something very different

The one independent, non-vendor academic audit that stress-tested a humanizer found GPTZero far weaker. Jabarian & Imas (BFI WP 2025-116, University of Chicago) pushed every AI passage through **StealthGPT's default rewrite endpoint** and re-scored four detectors. GPTZero's false-negative rate on humanized text ran **0.2552–0.7730 across genres and models**, clustering around 0.50–0.65 — i.e. roughly half of humanized passages passed. Originality.ai landed at 0.02–0.21; Pangram at 0.00–0.05 [9]. Masrour, Emi & Spero (DAMAGE) independently measured GPTZero dropping from **99.73% TPR on plain AI to 60.04% on humanized AI** in academic text, and 64.0%/61.0% on RAID's paraphrase/synonym attacks [4].

**Read for our purposes:** GPTZero's paraphrase class is real and is trained on humanizer output, but its recall on *good* humanizers is contested and plausibly 50–65%, not 93.5%. Pangram is the harder target. Any evaluation we run must include a Pangram-class detector or it is measuring the wrong thing.

The concrete, published list of what betrays humanized text. "Detected by" names the mechanism, not merely the vendor.

## 3. Countermeasure systems, one by one

### 3.1 GPTZero
Covered in §1. Two further items matter. **AI Patterns** (2026) is a public, explicitly enumerated taxonomy of ten AI rhetorical habits — Overblown Importance, Name Dropping, Empty Commentary, Sales-pitch Tone, Phantom Experts, Dressed-up Verbs, "Not just X, but Y", Everything in Threes, Unnecessary Caveats, Chatbot Language — computed as frequency multipliers over paired human/AI documents, and explicitly **not** fed into the score [23]. Like the AI Vocabulary feature it is interpretability, not decision logic. But it is a free, vendor-published checklist of exactly what a lexical humanizer fails to remove, and it should be implemented verbatim as one of our self-check modules. Second, GPTZero's **Internet Text Search** component verifies whether spans exist verbatim on the web, which is what makes artifact #15 (altered quotations) doubly dangerous: a mangled quotation is both an internal inconsistency and a failed external match.

### 3.2 Pangram / DAMAGE — the strongest published work
Masrour, Emi & Spero audited **19 tools**: paraphrasers DIPPER, Grammarly, QuillBot; humanizers BypassGPT, Ghost AI, HIX Bypass, Humbot AI, HumanizeAI.io, HumanizeAI.pro, Humanizer.com, Phrasly.ai, Semihuman AI, StealthGPT, StealthWriter.AI, Surfer SEO, Undetectable AI, Twixify, WriteHuman.ai [4]. They tier them by **faithfulness and fluency, explicitly not by evasion success**, using GPT-4o pairwise coherence win-rate vs the original:

- **L1** (win-rate 26.0%): DIPPER, GPTInf, Grammarly, HumanizeAI.pro, QuillBot, Semihuman AI, StealthGPT, Twixify — "rewrote text preserving its tone, vocabulary level, and complexity."
- **L2** (14.67%): AIHumanizer.com, BypassGPT, StealthWriter.AI, Surfer SEO — "degraded overall quality, but preserved intent and message."
- **L3** (2.67%): Ghost AI, HIX Bypass, HumanizeAI.io, Humbot AI, Phrasly, Undetectable AI, WriteHuman.ai — "added nonsensical phrases, words, and characters, constructed incorrect and uninterpretable sentences."

**Note that no tool wins the coherence comparison.** Even the best tier is judged worse than its input three times out of four. That is the quality bar we have to clear, and it is low.

The detector: Mistral NeMo 12B, frozen, LoRA adapters, untrained linear head, Tekken tokenizer, 512-token window, 8×A100, one epoch. The decisive trick is data-centric: humanization is treated as **a transform to which the model should be invariant**. They humanize *both* human and AI documents and label the humanized-human documents **human**. Humanized text is only 0.68% of the corpus, oversampled 18×. Result: 100.00% TPR on plain AI, **98.26% ± 0.47% on L1-humanized AI at 5% FPR**, and 93.2% TPR@5%FPR against an *adversarially fine-tuned* humanizer never seen in training [4]. Pangram's August 2025 public table shows 90.3–100% per-tool accuracy across 20 tools, with Undetectable AI lowest at 90.3% [14]; Pangram 4 adds a dedicated humanizer head at 96.82% accuracy over 13 commercial humanizers (per-tool TPR 91.52–99.39%) and 0.430% FNR against the 32k-star BLADER GitHub humanizer [2].

Pangram's own artifact list is the most concrete published: tortured phrases ("counterfeit consciousness"), spacing manipulation "to fool the tokenizer of the AI detectors," U+2009 thin spaces, and repetitive identical substitutions [14]; plus the slop-2 catalogue — hyphen-to-comma rewrites, spurious capitalisation, lost spaces after periods, tense mixing, tone-deaf synonyms, altered quotations, hallucinated facts [16].

### 3.3 Turnitin
Turnitin announced AI-bypasser detection on 27 August 2025. The technical content is one sentence from CPO Annie Chechitelli: they "researched and identified the signals and patterns of leading humanizers and have trained our model to identify them," because "humanizers also leave a statistical trace that can be learned and detected." Claimed document-level FPR under 1%; no independent verification, no per-tool numbers, no published artifact list [3]. Structurally it is the same move as GPTZero's and Pangram's: supervised training on humanizer output. Turnitin already reported detecting "AI-paraphrased" text before this. Its known operating parameters — 300-word minimum, overlapping few-hundred-word segments, sentence scores aggregated to a document percentage, no score shown in the 1–19% band, sentence-level FPR ≈4% concentrated at human/AI transitions — mean that **mixed-provenance documents are its weak spot and its trigger simultaneously**.

### 3.4 Originality.ai
Public per-humanizer table (September 2025 models): Undetectable.ai 80.3/92/82.4%, Phrasly.ai 86.1/92/89.0%, StealthWriter.ai 87.1/94/89.1%, Netus.ai 86.1/97/92.1%, Humbot.ai 81.2/90/83.3% for Lite 1.0.2 / Turbo 3.0.2 / Academic 0.0.5 [28]. No artifact taxonomy published. Independently, Originality's FNR on StealthGPT-humanized text was 0.02–0.21 depending on genre and generator [9]. Note that Originality collapses to ~9% accuracy under homoglyph attack on RAID while GPTZero does not — i.e. Unicode tricks help against Originality and actively hurt against GPTZero and Pangram. That asymmetry is a trap: an optimiser tuned on one detector will learn an artifact that another detector treats as a confession.

### 3.5 Copyleaks, Winston, and the rest
Neither publishes a humanizer-specific model, artifact list or benchmark that survives verification; Copyleaks' help centre would not render and Winston's public material describes only generic perplexity/burstiness/repetition analysis with a sentence-level "AI Prediction Map." Treat both as trailing indicators. Grammarly deserves a separate note: its "Humanize" feature launders content *through Grammarly's own provenance system*, producing an Authorship report reading "75% Typed by a Human" whose fine print says "0% typed by a human and 75% Rephrased with Grammarly's AI" [29]. That is a provenance-layer failure, not a text-layer one, and it will be closed.

Two results dominate the strategic picture. **Beemo** says the thing that actually works is *human editing*, and LLM editing is not a substitute [36]. **HART/Triospect/D2C** say that expression-only rewriting has a hard ceiling, because content origin is separately detectable [24][25][26]. Together they define the frontier: a text-only rewriter that changes expression without changing content is asymptotically detectable no matter how clean its artifacts are.

## 4. Collocation and naturalness metrics — concrete recipes

This is the self-check tooling for artifacts #1–#3, and the tortured-phrases literature hands us a validated method.

### 4.1 Masked-LM plausibility (the primary detector)

Martel, Lentschat & Labbé's tortured-phrase detector is directly reimplementable [11]. For each token, mask it and read three quantities from the MLM: **the probability of the original token, its rank in the predicted distribution, and the entropy of the distribution**. Token-level performance for tortured tokens: probability 0.32 P / 0.81 R; rank 0.34 P / 0.80 R. Then **propagate token scores to noun chunks** (a chunk is flagged if ≥1 of its tokens is flagged), which lifts it to **0.615 precision, 0.873 recall, 0.718 F1**. They used SciBERT; use a domain-matched MLM.

SRAP formalises the chunk score as a phrase log-likelihood [39]:

```
S_phrase = (1/N) · Σ_i log P(t_i | context; θ_MLM)
```

with a static anomaly threshold **T = −8.0**, empirically derived: valid scientific terms peak near **−4.5**, adversarial substitutions cluster below **−9.0**. SRAP's *restoration* stage only reaches 23.67% exact-match, which is a useful warning — an MLM can tell you a phrase is wrong but usually cannot tell you what it should have been. Detect, then reject and regenerate; do not try to auto-repair.

The right operationalisation for us is **ΔPLL, not absolute PLL**. Compute pseudo-log-likelihood [40] over the source span and the rewritten span; flag any span whose per-token PLL drops by more than a calibrated margin. This is scale-free and immune to the fact that some source text is legitimately unusual.

### 4.2 N-gram association against a reference corpus (the cheap gate)

Run first because it costs microseconds. From the Sketch Engine statistics reference [41], with N = corpus size, f_A/f_B = marginal frequencies, f_AB = co-occurrence:

- **logDice** = `14 + log₂( 2·f_AB / (f_A + f_B) )`. Rychlý's properties [42]: theoretical maximum 14, usually below 10; **0 means fewer than one co-occurrence per 16,000 occurrences of either word, and negative means no statistical association**; +1 point = twice as frequent, +7 points ≈ 100× as frequent; and critically **the score does not depend on corpus size**, so a single threshold transfers across reference corpora. This is the right primary measure.
- **MI (PMI)** = `log₂( f_AB·N / (f_A·f_B) )` — Church & Hanks 1990. Over-rewards rare pairs (Rychlý's BNC example returns "spell-wall", "hoodoo", "scapulum" as the top collocates of *break*), so use it only with a frequency floor.
- **t-score** = `(f_AB − f_A·f_B/N) / √f_AB` — over-rewards function words ("the", ".", ","). Useful as a sanity check, not a gate.
- **MI3** = `log₂( f_AB³·N / (f_A·f_B) )`; **log-likelihood** (Dunning 1993) = `2·(xlx(f_AB)+xlx(f_A−f_AB)+xlx(f_B−f_AB)+xlx(N)+xlx(N+f_AB−f_A−f_B)−xlx(f_A)−xlx(f_B)−xlx(N−f_A)−xlx(N−f_B))` where `xlx(f)=f·ln f`; **minimum sensitivity** = `min(f_AB/f_A, f_AB/f_B)`.

**Rule:** for every content bigram/trigram we introduce, require logDice ≥ 0 (no association is a red flag for a *substituted* pair even if it is fine for a novel one), and require that logDice does not fall by more than ~3 points relative to the source phrase it replaced.

**Reference corpora.** Google Books Ngram Export v3 (20200217) is the free workhorse: 1- through 5-grams, TSV `ngram TAB year TAB match_count TAB volume_count`, everything above 40 total occurrences, CC-BY-3.0 [43]. Its weaknesses — book-skewed, includes archaic usage — are fixable by restricting to years ≥ 1990. COCA n-grams (ngrams.info / wordfrequency.info) are genre-balanced and better for register-sensitive checks but are paid. ukWaC and the Sketch Engine corpora are the standard for logDice specifically. For anything domain-technical, build a local n-gram index from a corpus in that domain; a general corpus will flag legitimate jargon.

### 4.3 Static-embedding coherence (the free second opinion)

Martel et al. found that GloVe (`glove-wiki-gigaword-100`) cosine similarity between the tokens of a phrase separates tortured from expected phrases — **0.136 ± 0.157 vs 0.289 ± 0.201** — while contextual BERT embeddings do **not** (0.49 vs 0.51), because two-word inputs give BERT no context [10][11]. Counter-intuitive but useful: use *static* embeddings for phrase-internal coherence, *contextual* models for in-context plausibility. They are complementary and cheap.

### 4.4 Semantic-coherence and faithfulness checks

Bidirectional NLI entailment source↔output; embedding cosine (E5/SBERT) as a floor, never as the sole gate — report 03's finding stands, embedding similarity hides factual drift and broken collocations. Add hard set-equality gates on numerals, dates, units, named entities and citation-shaped spans, and byte-equality on quoted spans. TH-Bench's quality triple — perplexity, cosine/ROUGE-L, Flesch Reading Ease distance from the original — is a good ready-made panel [22].

## 5. Provenance and behavioural signals: what text can never fix

There is a class of evidence that lives outside the text, and no text-only tool touches it.

- **GPTZero** ships an **Origin Chrome extension** (8 support articles) and a **Microsoft Word add-in** (5 articles) alongside the core detector [44]. These are writing-process capture surfaces, not text classifiers.
- **Google Docs version history** is the default institutional evidence. Pangram's guidance is notable for its candour: students already fabricate it by manually typing AI essays, simulating edits, and running auto-typers, so version history "should not be used in isolation" [17].
- **Grammarly Authorship** attempts source attribution and fails in exactly the predictable place: pasted ChatGPT text is caught and attributed, but text *generated inside the document* shows as "unknown," and Grammarly's own Humanize pass relabels it "75% Typed by a Human" [29].
- **Keystroke timing is already broken as a defence.** Condrey (2026) shows automated agents reach **≥99.8% evasion** by sampling inter-keystroke intervals from human distributions, and states the limitation exactly: such systems "confirm a human operated the keyboard, but not whether that human originated the text" [45].
- **Rich process datasets now exist**: ScholaWrite (~62k text changes over four months of real Overleaf keystrokes with cognitive annotations) [46], NIRVANA (77 students, keystroke-level behaviour plus full ChatGPT conversation histories and copied content, with a replay interface) [47], IRIS (keystroke logs mapped to a cognitive model of writing) [48], and AITDNA (full edit and AI-interaction histories) [49]. These are training corpora for process-level detectors.

**What we must tell users honestly.** A text-only tool cannot produce a paste-free edit history, cannot produce plausible keystroke timing, cannot produce a Docs revision trail with real pauses and backtracking, and cannot make Grammarly Authorship or GPTZero Origin report human authorship. If a workflow is instrumented at the process layer, output quality is irrelevant to the outcome. Anyone who claims otherwise is selling something. The correct positioning is: we improve the *text*, and the text is only one of several channels.

## 6. The "human first draft" target

Our aim is output that looks like a genuine human draft, not laundered AI. Three findings shape what that means.

**Human writing is 25% revision.** ScholaWrite's annotated keystroke corpus splits real scholarly writing into Planning 9.7%, Implementation 65.0%, Revision 25.3% of time, with Text Production alone at 57.4% of all actions and Clarity revisions at 11.5%. Planning and citation integration front-load; structural revision intensifies near submission; Idea Organization appears *only* early. State-of-the-art LLMs score 0.41 F1 at predicting these intentions and "remain weak at sustaining intertwined reasoning processes" [46]. The signature of a real draft is therefore **local unevenness that reflects a revision history**: some paragraphs polished, some not; a clarity fix in one sentence and an unfixed clumsy one three sentences later; an idea introduced early and reorganised late. Uniform polish across a document is the single most machine-like global property, and it is exactly what a one-pass humanizer produces.

**Expert human editing evades detection; LLM editing does not.** Beemo's core result [36]. Whatever expert editors do is qualitatively different from what an LLM does when told to edit, and the difference is measurable by 33 detector configurations. This is the strongest available argument for a pipeline that makes *selective, targeted, uneven* edits rather than a uniform rewrite pass.

**Errors must be the right shape or absent.** Perkins et al. found spelling-error injection was the most effective 2024 adversarial technique (detector accuracy 39.5% → 17.4%) while noting the output "would very unlikely be submitted by a student" [12]. Genuine first-draft errors are homophone confusions, agreement slips, a dropped article, a comma splice, an inconsistent hyphenation choice — not "recieves" and "easly" sprinkled at random [16]. Given that detectors now treat error injection as a paraphraser signal, the safe policy is **zero injected errors** and a LanguageTool rate inside the genre band, achieved by not over-polishing rather than by adding noise.

**One structural escape hatch.** "Base Models Look Human To AI Detectors" reports that GPTZero and Pangram judge *base*-model text as overwhelmingly human while instruction-tuned output is flagged, concluding detectors track "artifacts of instruction tuning" [38]. This supports report 05's Stage-1 recommendation to start from a base checkpoint. It is also a moving target: it describes a gap in current training data, and DAMAGE-style augmentation closes exactly that kind of gap.

## 7. The humanizer-artifact self-check suite: what our pipeline must validate before returning output

Every output passes all of these or is rejected and regenerated. Cheap checks first; they will catch most failures for free.

**Tier 0 — Byte and character assertions (microseconds; hard fail).**
1. Output is NFC-normalised and contains no character outside the input's character set plus standard ASCII punctuation. Zero U+2009, U+200B/C/D, no Cyrillic/Greek homoglyphs in Latin words.
2. Exactly one space after terminal punctuation; no double spaces; no missing space after a period.
3. Hyphen count within ±20% of source; no source hyphen replaced by a comma; no date range converted to a comma pair.
4. Capitalisation only at sentence starts and on NER-confirmed proper nouns; mid-sentence capitals in the output must exist in the input.
5. No non-linguistic tokens: code identifiers, `(??????)`, stray page ranges, bracketed placeholders.

**Tier 1 — Faithfulness gates (hard fail).**
6. Set of numerals, dates, percentages, units and named entities is identical between input and output.
7. Quoted spans byte-identical to source.
8. Citation-shaped spans in output ⊆ citation-shaped spans in input. Zero new citations.
9. Bidirectional NLI entailment above threshold in both directions; E5/SBERT cosine floor as a secondary check only.
10. Length ratio within ±25%; sentence-count delta within a small band; no unaligned source proposition.

**Tier 2 — Naturalness gates (§4).**
11. For every changed content bigram/trigram: logDice ≥ 0 against the reference corpus, and Δ logDice vs the replaced phrase ≥ −3.
12. Masked-LM chunk score above the calibrated floor (SRAP-style, ≈ −8.0 with SciBERT on scientific text; recalibrate per domain and MLM). Flag chunks Martel-style — mask each token, take probability / rank / entropy, propagate to noun chunks.
13. ΔPLL per token vs the source span within a calibrated margin.
14. GloVe cosine between tokens of any newly created multiword expression above the tortured-phrase band (target ≥ 0.29-ish, reject near 0.14).
15. No low-frequency n-gram introduced more than once in the document.
16. Document perplexity inside the genre's human band, and no sentence in the extreme tail.

**Tier 3 — Consistency and register gates.**
17. Tense consistency within each paragraph; no unlicensed past-perfect/simple-past mixing.
18. Every pronoun binds to an antecedent under coreference resolution; no pronoun introduced that has no referent.
19. Formality and readability of the output within a narrow band of the input, per genre. No elementary-level rewrite of academic text and no academic inflation of casual text.
20. Contraction rate and sentence-fragment rate inside the genre's human band, not merely "higher than the input."
21. LanguageTool error rate inside the human band from **both** sides — not zero, not inflated, and no nonword misspellings.

**Tier 4 — Residue and rhetoric.**
22. GPTZero's ten AI Patterns implemented as detectors — Overblown Importance, Name Dropping, Empty Commentary, Sales-pitch Tone, Phantom Experts, Dressed-up Verbs, "Not just X, but Y", Everything in Threes, Unnecessary Caveats, Chatbot Language — each below its genre-conditioned human rate. These survive lexical rewriting and must be removed rhetorically.
23. Sentence-length distribution matched to the genre's human distribution (shape, not just variance); explicitly reject the all-short-clauses failure mode.
24. Polish *unevenness*: reject documents whose per-paragraph quality/formality metrics are suspiciously uniform.

**Tier 5 — Adversarial self-evaluation (the one that actually matters).**
25. Score against a detector ensemble that includes at least one **humanizer-aware** model. GPTZero's paraphrase class and Pangram's humanizer head are the live targets; internally, train a DAMAGE-style classifier **on our own pipeline's output** and hold it out of the reward. If our own discriminator can separate our output from genuine human text, so can theirs.
26. LLM-judge pairwise coherence vs the unmodified input, win-rate ≥ 50%. Every audited humanizer fails this: 26.0% for the best tier, 2.67% for the worst.
27. Content-origin check: run a HART-style neutralisation (reduce to an outline, detect on the outline). If the outline is still classified AI, the rewrite has only changed expression, and we should say so rather than pretend otherwise.
28. Style-embedding distance (LUAR / StyleDistance) to the human genre centroid, not just detector score — detector-only optimisation produces detector-shaped text.

If a document fails Tier 5 item 27, the honest product behaviour is to tell the user that expression has been improved but the content fingerprint remains, rather than to ship a document that will be labelled "AI (Paraphrased)."
